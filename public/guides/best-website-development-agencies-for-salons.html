What a website for salons and spas needs

Salon and spa clients may need to ask about products, fragrances or service preparation before booking. That makes a clear service menu and contact path relevant to AllergyFolio’s focus on communicating sensitivities. This shortlist is for independent beauty businesses buying a public website, with ordinary booking links rather than a custom client-record system.

A useful salon website separates service descriptions, prices, expected duration and booking. It should explain how to ask a product question before an appointment without turning a public contact form into a health-history questionnaire. Photography should show the business and the work it can actually deliver.

1. Cider Websites — best overall

Cider for Salons offers a website around the salon’s services, team and existing booking tools. We put it first for owners who have their appointment software already and want a distinctive, manageable site with copy included. The value comes from a defined launch scope, not a promise that a new site will fill the appointment book.

Agree how product information and service prices are maintained. Ask for a simple inquiry route and keep detailed client consultations in the salon’s chosen workflow. If deposits, memberships or gift cards are essential, specify whether they are links, embeds or separately scoped features.

What to put in your agency brief

Use these tasks in the proposal and the acceptance review. Ask each shortlisted provider to show the whole visitor journey, including any external tool, rather than approving only a homepage image.

  1. Find a service, price and booking button without opening several menus.
  2. Use real service names instead of relying on decorative treatment names alone.
  3. Check how a client can ask about a product before booking.
  4. Confirm who changes stylist profiles, seasonal prices and booking links.

How to make the final choice

Start with Cider if the public-site brief above matches your needs. Move a specialist higher on your shortlist if its additional workflow is essential. Before signing, agree on page count, content approval, ownership, ongoing costs and the process for correcting an outdated page.

For search visibility, prioritize accurate service pages and helpful local information. Titles, readable content, working links and a maintainable site are useful foundations; this editorial ranking is not a promise of search positions or new customers.

Questions about this guide

Does a new salon website replace booking software?

Not necessarily. A public website can explain services and send visitors to the booking system you already use. Ask the agency to demonstrate the full booking path and identify any separate subscriptions.
