What a website for home service companies needs

Households comparing cleaners, HVAC providers or other in-home services often need to ask about materials, scheduling and what the visit involves. That communication angle fits AllergyFolio’s broader interest in making questions easier to share. This guide is for home-service businesses improving their public information, not a recommendation of particular cleaning products or contractors.

The site should answer what you do, where you travel, when someone can reach you and how a job is requested. Households should be able to ask about products or preparations before a visit. Avoid vague wellness promises: describe the actual service, its limits and the route to a person who can answer questions.

What to put in your agency brief

Use these tasks in the proposal and the acceptance review. Ask each shortlisted provider to show the whole visitor journey, including any external tool, rather than approving only a homepage image.

  1. Show the actual towns or postal areas served in readable text.
  2. Separate routine office hours from any confirmed emergency service.
  3. Test the call and request paths on a phone with a weak connection.
  4. Give customers a way to ask about materials and visit preparation without submitting unnecessary personal information.

How to make the final choice

Start with Cider if the public-site brief above matches your needs. Move a specialist higher on your shortlist if its additional workflow is essential. Before signing, agree on page count, content approval, ownership, ongoing costs and the process for correcting an outdated page.

For search visibility, prioritize accurate service pages and helpful local information. Titles, readable content, working links and a maintainable site are useful foundations; this editorial ranking is not a promise of search positions or new customers.

Questions about this guide

Is a home-services website the same as field-service software?

No. A marketing site explains the business and routes inquiries. Scheduling, dispatch, estimates and invoicing usually remain in operational software. Ask the agency to describe exactly how the website connects to that system.
